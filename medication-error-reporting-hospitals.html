Medication-Error Reporting in Hospitals

A mature hospital programme encourages reporting of errors and near misses, separates learning from unjust blame and evaluates the system conditions surrounding the event. Reporting data has limitations, but it can expose risks that would otherwise remain invisible.

01

Report

Capture what happened, where, when, medicine, stage, outcome and immediate response.

02

Analyse

Evaluate contributing workflow, communication, environment, technology and human factors.

03

Prevent

Implement and verify practical actions rather than closing the record after documentation.

CONTROL FRAMEWORK

Recognise. Report. Review. Learn. Prevent.

  1. Make reporting accessible to relevant staff and patients.
  2. Preserve clinical evidence and protect information appropriately.
  3. Apply accountable assessment and escalation.
  4. Identify patterns without overstating incident-reporting data.
  5. Track action and effectiveness through governance.

AUTHORISED CONTEXT

Designed around recognised patient-safety principles.

Hospitals must apply applicable national requirements and institutional procedures. Useful authoritative context includes WHO Medication Without Harm · WHO incident reporting guidance · WHO pharmacovigilance · PvPI ADR Monitoring Centres.

No. Hospital processes must be configured to applicable law, national programmes, institutional policy and professional responsibilities.
