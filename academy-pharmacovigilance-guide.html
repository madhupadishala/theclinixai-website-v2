01 / CAREER

How to start a career in Pharmacovigilance.

For a fresher, the first step is understanding the safety lifecycle rather than memorising isolated definitions. Entry-level work can involve receiving safety information, checking whether a report is valid, entering structured data, coding medical information, writing a clear case narrative, performing quality checks and supporting follow-up.

But PV is not only case processing. As professionals develop, the field can extend into literature surveillance, quality management, aggregate safety reporting, signal detection and management, regulatory compliance, medical review and safety strategy.

02 / TRAINING

What should a serious PV training programme cover?

A useful pharmacovigilance course, institute, internship or apprenticeship should connect the terminology to the work. The exact depth varies by programme, but the learning map below represents the language candidates and employers commonly use when discussing PV capability.

Core PV

Pharmacovigilance fundamentals

Adverse events, adverse drug reactions, seriousness, severity, expectedness, causality, special situations, reporting concepts and patient safety.

Case processing

ICSR processing

Individual Case Safety Report workflows from intake through triage, validity, assessment, data entry, narrative, QC, follow-up and closure.

Coding

MedDRA and medical coding

MedDRA hierarchy, terminology selection, adverse event coding, indication, medical history and drug-related coding concepts.

Quality

Review and quality control

Completeness, consistency, source-to-case traceability, narrative quality, review comments, corrections and controlled documentation.

Advanced PV

Signals and aggregate safety

Signal detection and assessment concepts plus PSUR/PBRER/PADER/DSUR and risk-management fundamentals.

Regulatory

Regulatory pharmacovigilance

ICH concepts, regional reporting expectations, expedited reporting, compliance, timelines and the relationship between operations and regulatory obligations.

03 / ICSR

ICSR processing is a workflow, not just data entry.

Individual Case Safety Reports are a major foundation of entry-level drug safety work. A strong learner should understand why each step exists and how one decision affects the rest of the case.

That includes case intake, source review, minimum criteria, duplicate assessment, seriousness, expectedness, causality, medical coding, narrative writing, quality control, follow-up and closure. Software can support the workflow, but the professional still needs to understand the safety reasoning behind the fields.

Intake & triage

  • Source identification
  • Initial safety information review
  • Case triage
  • Minimum criteria and validity
  • Duplicate considerations

Case assessment

  • Seriousness assessment
  • Severity versus seriousness
  • Expectedness/listedness concepts
  • Causality assessment
  • Special situations

Case completion

  • MedDRA coding
  • Drug coding
  • Narrative writing
  • QC and medical review
  • Follow-up and closure

04 / MEDDRA

MedDRA matters because PV needs a common language.

MedDRA is used to standardise medical terminology in safety information. Learning it should go beyond memorising hierarchy names. Candidates need to understand how the reported medical concept is translated into standardised terminology and how coding choices affect downstream analysis.

05 / LITERATURE

Literature surveillance connects evidence to safety work.

Pharmacovigilance literature screening can involve searching scientific sources, reviewing titles and abstracts, assessing relevance, retrieving full text, identifying potential safety cases and documenting decisions. A modern workflow also needs to distinguish AI-assisted screening from accountable human review.

What learners should understand

Search strategy, screening, relevance, full-text review, case identification, duplicate handling, documentation and escalation.

Where AI can help

Prioritisation, extraction, classification and workflow support—while keeping traceability, review and decision accountability with the appropriate human process.

06 / SIGNALS

Beyond cases: understanding the safety signal.

Signal detection

Understand why individual cases can contribute to a broader pattern and how quantitative or qualitative approaches may support signal identification.

Signal validation

Understand the difference between an observation and a validated safety signal, including the need for evidence review and clinical context.

Signal assessment

Understand case series, literature, medical evidence, regulatory context and benefit-risk considerations used in signal evaluation.

07 / AGGREGATE SAFETY

PV careers do not stop at individual cases.

Aggregate safety reporting brings information together across a defined period to support ongoing safety evaluation and regulatory communication. Learners should understand the purpose and structure of major report types rather than treating them as unexplained acronyms.

08 / REGULATORY

Regulatory knowledge gives the workflow a purpose.

ICH

Understand the role of ICH safety guidance and the relationship between global standards and operational PV activities.

Regional requirements

Understand that reporting rules, timelines and source requirements can vary by market and regulator.

Compliance mindset

Documentation, auditability, controlled processes, timelines and traceability are part of professional PV—not administrative extras.

11 / QUESTIONS CANDIDATES ASK

Pharmacovigilance training FAQs.

How do I start a career in pharmacovigilance?

Build the fundamentals first, then develop practical capability in ICSR processing, medical terminology, MedDRA, narrative writing, quality review, literature surveillance and regulatory concepts. Case-based evidence of what you can perform can strengthen the transition from academic learning to entry-level work.

What should I look for when comparing PV institutes or courses?

Look beyond the topic list. Check whether the programme explains the actual workflow, provides meaningful practice, gives feedback and assessment, teaches quality and documentation, and is transparent about what participants will and will not access.

Is ICSR processing enough?

It is an important entry-level foundation, but PV extends into literature surveillance, quality, signal management, aggregate safety reporting, regulatory compliance and medical review.

Do I need to know Argus or another safety database?

Understanding how a safety database supports the case lifecycle is valuable. Specific software exposure depends on the programme and the access actually provided. A training provider should not imply access it does not provide.

Is a pharmacovigilance internship different from a course?

It can be. An internship generally implies practical exposure over a defined period, while a course may be primarily instructional. The label alone does not prove the depth of practical work, so candidates should inspect the actual activities, supervision and assessment.

Does TheClinixAI Academy guarantee a PV job?

No. The Academy does not sell jobs or guarantee placement. It focuses on practical capability, documented learning and professional readiness.
