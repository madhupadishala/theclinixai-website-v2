01 / CAREER

How to start a career in Pharmacovigilance.

Pharmacovigilance, or drug safety, is the science and practice of monitoring and evaluating medicine safety. For a new graduate, the practical starting point is understanding how safety information moves from a source to a documented safety decision.

Entry-level work can include safety information intake, case triage, validity checks, data entry, medical coding, narrative writing, follow-up, quality control and reporting support. With experience, professionals can move toward literature surveillance, aggregate safety, signal management, medical review, compliance, quality, risk management and safety leadership.

02 / ELIGIBILITY

Who can build a career in pharmacovigilance?

Eligibility is employer- and role-specific. Pharmacy and pharmaceutical sciences are common routes, while medicine, life sciences, nursing and related scientific or healthcare backgrounds can also be relevant. Government programmes may set their own criteria; the National Coordination Centre–PvPI internship, for example, has included pharmaceutical, medical, dental, nursing and allied health disciplines.

Pharmacy

B.Pharm / M.Pharm / Pharm.D

Strong foundation for therapeutics, medicines, adverse events and safety concepts.

Life sciences

B.Sc / M.Sc / Biotechnology / Microbiology

Relevant for roles where the employer accepts scientific backgrounds.

Healthcare

Medicine / Nursing / Allied sciences

Clinical knowledge can support case understanding and medically focused roles.

05 / ICSR PROCESSING

ICSR processing is a workflow, not just data entry.

Individual Case Safety Reports are a major foundation of entry-level drug safety work. A strong learner should understand why each step exists and how one decision affects the rest of the case.

Typical learning includes source review, minimum criteria and validity, duplicate assessment, seriousness, severity, expectedness/listedness, causality, medical coding, narrative writing, quality control, follow-up and closure.

06 / MEDDRA

MedDRA: learning the language of safety data.

MedDRA is a standardised medical terminology used to code and organise medical information for regulatory and safety purposes. Good training should explain the hierarchy and how a reported medical concept is translated into an appropriate term.

08 / LITERATURE

Literature surveillance connects scientific evidence to safety work.

Literature surveillance can involve searching scientific sources, screening records, assessing relevance, retrieving full text, identifying potential safety information and documenting decisions. It is a major opportunity for modern PV professionals because AI can support prioritisation and extraction while accountable human review remains essential.

What learners should understand

Search strategy, screening, relevance, full-text review, case identification, duplicates, documentation and escalation.

AI-assisted workflow

Prioritisation, extraction and classification can be assisted by AI, but evidence inspection, review and decisions must remain appropriately governed.

11 / REGULATORY PV

Regulatory knowledge gives the workflow a purpose.

Professional PV operates within applicable regulatory expectations and controlled company procedures. Learners should understand the relationship between operational activities, reporting obligations, timelines, documentation, traceability and compliance. Regional requirements vary, so current authority guidance and employer procedures must always be checked.

ICH

Understand the role of international safety guidance and how it informs PV activities.

Regional requirements

Understand why reporting rules, timelines and source requirements can differ by market.

Compliance mindset

Auditability, controlled processes, documentation and traceability are professional PV responsibilities.

Clinical-trial safety

Post-marketing PV

13 / MEDICAL REVIEW

Medical review is where safety information meets clinical judgement.

Medical review may involve assessment of clinical information, seriousness, relatedness, expectedness and other medically relevant considerations. The exact responsibility depends on the organisation, role and governance model.

For students, the important lesson is that PV is not merely administrative data entry. Strong case processing requires understanding the medical story represented by the data.

14 / AI + PV

The next PV professional needs responsible AI literacy.

AI can support literature prioritisation, extraction, case intake, duplicate detection, coding assistance, narrative drafting, quality checks and signal analytics. Regulated safety work still requires evidence, traceability, appropriate validation, human review and accountability.

Useful AI exposure

Prioritisation, extraction, classification, summarisation, workflow routing and evidence organisation.

Professional responsibility

Source verification, uncertainty awareness, review, documentation and accountable safety decisions.

15 / INTERVIEWS

PV interview preparation should test reasoning, not memorisation.

Students commonly prepare for questions on ICSR, MedDRA, seriousness, severity, expectedness, causality, narrative writing, follow-up, signal detection, aggregate reports and regulatory concepts. Strong preparation also includes scenarios involving missing information, duplicates, serious cases, unclear causality and QC findings.

Fresher questions

PV basics, AE vs ADR, seriousness vs severity, minimum criteria, ICSR lifecycle and MedDRA hierarchy.

Scenario questions

Missing information, duplicate cases, follow-up, causality, special situations and conflicting source information.

Advanced questions

Signals, aggregate reporting, benefit-risk, literature, quality systems and AI-enabled PV.

17 / STUDENT FAQs

Questions to ask before choosing a PV programme.

Is pharmacovigilance a good career for freshers?

It can be a relevant career path for candidates with suitable scientific or healthcare backgrounds who develop PV knowledge, accuracy, documentation discipline and practical skills. Outcomes depend on the candidate, employer and market.

Is a certificate enough to get a PV job?

A certificate documents course completion but does not by itself demonstrate that a candidate can perform PV work. Employers may assess technical knowledge, communication, accuracy and practical understanding.

Should I choose a course, internship or apprenticeship?

Choose according to your objective. A course may focus on structured knowledge; an internship may add practical exposure; an apprenticeship emphasises supervised practice, feedback and progressive competency. Inspect the actual activities rather than the label.

Do I need Argus to enter pharmacovigilance?

Not necessarily. Safety database exposure can be useful, but core PV reasoning, data quality and process discipline are transferable across systems.

What should I learn first: MedDRA, ICSR or Argus?

Start with PV concepts and the safety workflow, then ICSR and coding, followed by the system relevant to your target role. Software is easier to understand when the underlying process is clear.

Can pharmacy and life-science graduates enter PV?

Often yes, where the employer's eligibility criteria permit. Always check the specific job description or programme requirements.
