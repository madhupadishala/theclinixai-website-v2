PV Agreements & Partner Governance

Pharmacovigilance Agreements and Partner Governance: A Complete Compliance Guide

A complete guide to PV agreements, SDEAs, partner responsibilities, case exchange, reconciliation, vendor oversight and MAH accountability.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-235 min read

A patient reports a serious adverse event to a distributor. The distributor logs it as a product complaint and forwards it to the manufacturer ten days later. The manufacturer assumes the licensing partner is responsible. The partner never receives it.

Every organisation can point to a contract. None can prove that the safety information reached the accountable pharmacovigilance system on time.

This is not primarily a case-processing failure. It is a governance failure created at the interface between organisations.

Patient safety does not recognise corporate boundaries. The regulatory clock will not pause while partners debate ownership.

Why partner governance is a core PV control

Medicinal products can involve MAHs, licence partners, manufacturers, distributors, wholesalers, affiliates, CROs, call centres, digital vendors and service providers. Safety information may enter through any of them.

The PV system must define how information is identified, transferred, processed, submitted, reconciled and escalated. Contractual language supports this control, but the real objective is uninterrupted safety oversight.

Delegation does not remove accountability

An MAH may subcontract pharmacovigilance activities, but it retains responsibility for its PV system and obligations. This principle should govern every outsourcing and licensing decision.

The responsible organisation must know what was delegated, to whom, for which products and territories, under which standards, and how performance is independently verified.

From commercial contract to operational safety agreement

Commercial agreements often define ownership, supply, territories and payment while leaving PV responsibilities vague. A dedicated safety data exchange agreement or equivalent PV section translates the relationship into operational controls.

The agreement must align with regulatory obligations, product lifecycle, countries, data sources and actual systems. Generic clauses copied across partnerships are rarely sufficient.

Essential responsibility domains

A complete responsibility matrix should address every safety-critical activity rather than focusing only on spontaneous cases.

  • Safety-information intake and employee awareness.
  • Case transfer, follow-up, duplicate management and medical review.
  • Literature monitoring and local-language sources.
  • Regulatory submission ownership and acknowledgements.
  • Clinical-trial and post-marketing boundaries.
  • Signal detection, validation, assessment and communication.
  • Aggregate reports, RMPs and safety variations.
  • Reconciliation, compliance metrics, deviations and CAPA.
  • Inspection, audit, business continuity and termination support.

Awareness date and exchange timelines

The agreement should define when each party is considered aware, which minimum information triggers transfer, and whether clocks run in calendar or business days. Internal timelines should preserve sufficient time for assessment and submission.

A transfer deadline that is technically within a regulatory timeline may still be operationally unsafe if it leaves the receiver no time for duplicate search, medical review or transmission.

Product, territory and source scope

Scope should list products, active substances, formulations, indications, studies, countries and legal entities. It should define spontaneous, solicited, literature, digital, social-media, medical-information, complaint and market-research sources where applicable.

Ambiguity is dangerous during acquisition, divestment, co-marketing or indication expansion because the operational scope can change before the agreement is updated.

Reconciliation as proof of completeness

Reconciliation is the control that tests whether expected information crossed the interface. It should compare both directions, resolve discrepancies, assess lateness and document closure.

A statement that 'no cases were exchanged' is not reconciliation unless each party confirms its source population, period and search method.

Oversight, metrics and governance meetings

Governance should review exchange timeliness, rejected files, quality defects, missing follow-up, reconciliation discrepancies, overdue actions, deviations and regulatory impact. Meeting attendance is not evidence of oversight.

Thresholds should trigger escalation and CAPA. Repeat late exchange should be treated as a system weakness, not a monthly operational inconvenience.

Change control

Regulatory change, new product, new territory, new vendor, system migration, merger, divestment or contact change can invalidate an agreement. Each relationship needs a change-notification mechanism and accountable owner.

The effective operational process and contract must never drift apart silently.

Termination and data transition

Termination clauses should address case cut-off, open follow-up, database transfer, source documents, submissions, signals, aggregate reports, RMP commitments, records, retention, privacy and inspection support.

A commercial exit does not erase historical pharmacovigilance obligations.

A practical failure scenario

A service provider reports 100% on-time exchange because it measures delivery from its safety mailbox. During audit, the MAH discovers that local medical-information teams sometimes hold reports for internal clarification before sending them to that mailbox. The metric is correct only after the provider receives the case; the uncontrolled time before receipt is invisible.

The solution requires enterprise training, source mapping, day-zero governance, agreement clarification, retrospective impact assessment and a metric beginning at first organisational awareness—not a reminder to the vendor.

Technology and AI across partners

Structured gateways and APIs can reduce manual delay, but mappings, acknowledgements, error queues and version compatibility require validation and monitoring. AI can classify incoming communications or detect missing data, but the parties must define accountability for false negatives, evidence retention and human review.

No partner should assume that an automated transfer was successful until the receiving system confirms acceptance.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • All products, territories and entities are scoped.
  • Responsibilities cover the full PV lifecycle.
  • Awareness and transfer clocks are unambiguous.
  • Submission and acknowledgement ownership are defined.
  • Reconciliation is bidirectional and evidenced.
  • Metrics begin at the correct awareness point.
  • Changes trigger controlled agreement review.
  • Deviations and CAPA cross organisational boundaries.
  • Termination protects ongoing and historical obligations.
  • MAH oversight remains documented.

Frequently asked questions

What is a pharmacovigilance agreement?

A controlled agreement defining how parties identify, exchange, process, submit, reconcile and govern safety information and related PV obligations.

Is an SDEA always legally required as a document with that name?

Requirements and contracting models vary, but delegated and shared PV responsibilities must be clearly defined and controlled. A dedicated SDEA is a common operational mechanism.

Can an MAH outsource responsibility?

It can outsource activities, but it retains accountability for its PV system and regulatory obligations.

What should be reconciled?

Cases, transfers, submissions and other governed safety deliverables according to the relationship’s scope and risk.

When should an agreement be revised?

When products, territories, entities, processes, systems, regulations or delegated responsibilities materially change.

Conclusion

The strongest pharmacovigilance agreement is not the longest. It is the one that prevents a safety report, signal or regulatory obligation from disappearing between organisations.

Contracts define responsibility. Governance proves that responsibility is being performed.

If every partner believes someone else owns the clock, the patient is the only person left carrying the risk.
