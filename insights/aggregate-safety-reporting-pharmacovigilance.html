Aggregate Safety Reporting

Aggregate Safety Reporting in Pharmacovigilance: PBRER, PSUR and DSUR

A complete guide to aggregate safety reporting, including PBRER, PSUR, DSUR, data lock, signal integration and benefit–risk evaluation.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-235 min read

An individual case asks, “What happened to this patient?” An aggregate report asks a harder question: “What does everything we now know mean for all patients exposed to this medicine?”

That question cannot be answered by compiling tables, copying the previous report or listing signals without interpreting them.

Aggregate reporting is not a periodic document-production exercise. It is the scheduled scientific reassessment of whether the medicine’s benefits continue to outweigh its risks.

Regulatory foundation: ICH E2C(R2); ICH E2C Q&A; GVP VII

What aggregate safety reporting covers

Aggregate reports integrate cumulative and interval safety information across cases, studies, literature, signals, regulatory actions, exposure and important efficacy developments. The report type depends on product lifecycle and jurisdiction.

PBRER and PSUR terminology is often used in marketed-product contexts; DSUR addresses investigational products in development. Their data locks, content and regulatory obligations are not interchangeable.

The evidence architecture

A defensible report requires governed datasets and traceability from source to table to conclusion. Case counts alone are insufficient without exposure, reporting context, duplicates, coding strategy and medically meaningful grouping.

  • Worldwide marketing-authorisation status and regulatory actions
  • Estimated exposure and limitations
  • Interval and cumulative case data
  • Study and literature findings
  • Signal evaluations and risk-management actions
  • Benefit information and therapeutic context
  • Integrated benefit–risk analysis

PBRER/PSUR scientific logic

ICH E2C(R2) places benefit–risk evaluation at the centre. Sections should not function as isolated summaries. New safety information must be interpreted in light of cumulative knowledge, exposure, indication, population and available alternatives.

DSUR scientific logic

The DSUR focuses on the ongoing safety of subjects exposed during development. It integrates serious adverse reactions, important risks, study changes, non-clinical information, regulatory actions and the sponsor’s assessment of whether trials can continue safely.

Data lock, authoring and governance

Planning should begin before the data lock point. Responsibilities, source owners, query specifications, reconciliation, signal cut-offs, review cycles and approval must be defined. Late discovery of data inconsistency is a governance failure, not an authoring inconvenience.

Benefit–risk evaluation

The conclusion should identify key benefits, key risks, uncertainties, important changes and whether additional pharmacovigilance or risk minimisation is required. A favourable conclusion must be reasoned—not inherited from the previous reporting period.

Automation and AI

Automation can generate validated listings, exposure tables, literature inventories and consistency checks. AI can support evidence synthesis and draft comparisons, but cannot autonomously decide benefit–risk balance.

Every generated statement should trace to governed evidence, effective data cuts and accountable scientific review.

The scientific questions every aggregate team must answer

The reporting period should be interrogated, not merely described. What changed in exposure? Which events increased or changed clinically? Did new studies alter understanding of a risk or benefit? Did a signal assessment change the safety specification? Did regulatory action in one country create implications elsewhere? Did risk-minimisation data show that an intervention worked—or failed?

A useful report distinguishes new information from new interpretation. Sometimes no new event is identified, yet cumulative evidence changes severity, frequency, susceptible population, preventability or clinical management. That change can be as important as discovery of a new reaction.

  • Explain important interval changes against cumulative context.
  • State limitations of exposure and case ascertainment.
  • Reconcile signal conclusions with RMP and product information.
  • Identify concrete actions arising from the evaluation.

Authoring governance and cross-functional accountability

Aggregate reports are assembled across safety operations, medical review, biostatistics, epidemiology, clinical development, regulatory affairs, labelling and risk management. The author should not be forced to discover at final review that these functions used different data cuts or product scopes.

A controlled source matrix should define each input, system of record, owner, cut-off, format, reconciliation requirement and approval. Review comments should be resolved with documented rationale, especially when they change risk classification or benefit–risk conclusions.

  • Freeze governed datasets before final medical interpretation.
  • Use one controlled source for repeated numbers.
  • Record material judgement changes between drafts.
  • Link conclusions to follow-up actions and accountable owners.

A practical decision scenario

Consider a reporting period in which spontaneous case counts for a known risk rise by 60%. The increase appears alarming until exposure data show a major market expansion and a communication campaign that stimulated reporting. At the same time, the proportion of cases involving hospitalisation has increased in one vulnerable population. A strong aggregate report does not choose between 'more reports' and 'no change.' It separates reporting volume from clinical pattern, evaluates population-specific severity and determines whether targeted risk action is required.

The governance lesson extends beyond the example. A decision should never depend on one isolated metric, one source or one reviewer’s unrecorded intuition. The complete evidence chain—from detection or data extraction through medical interpretation, cross-functional challenge, approval and action—must remain visible. This is how a pharmacovigilance system becomes both scientifically credible and inspection-ready.

TheClinixAI position is deliberately firm: automation should accelerate retrieval, reconciliation and consistency; it should not erase uncertainty or manufacture consensus. When evidence conflicts, the system must show the conflict. When a human overrides an automated recommendation, the rationale must remain attributable. Patient-safety intelligence becomes trustworthy only when speed and traceability improve together.

What enterprise-grade execution adds

The aggregate strategy must also recognise regional schedules and formats. A global core evaluation may support several submissions, but local requirements, reporting frequencies, product scopes and appendices can differ. Reuse is valuable only when regional obligations remain visible and controlled.

Medical writing quality depends on decision architecture. Reviewers should know which sections are descriptive, which are analytical and which carry conclusions that can change labelling, studies, pharmacovigilance plans or risk minimisation. Review effort should follow scientific consequence, not distribute equally across every page.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Correct report type and data lock are confirmed.
  • Sources and owners are predefined.
  • Interval and cumulative datasets reconcile.
  • Exposure assumptions are explicit.
  • Signals and regulatory actions are current.
  • Benefits and risks are evaluated together.
  • Conclusions identify uncertainty and action.
  • Submission and acknowledgement evidence is retained.

Frequently asked questions

What is aggregate safety reporting?

It is periodic cumulative evaluation of safety, exposure, benefits, signals and regulatory information for a medicinal product.

What is the difference between PBRER and DSUR?

PBRER focuses on marketed-product benefit–risk evaluation; DSUR focuses on investigational-product safety during development.

Is a PSUR only a case-count report?

No. It requires integrated scientific evaluation, not merely listings and summary tabulations.

Can AI author an aggregate report?

It can assist with governed synthesis and consistency, but accountable experts must own interpretation and conclusions.

Conclusion

A strong aggregate report does not hide uncertainty behind volume. It shows what changed, why it matters and what action follows.

If the conclusion could have been written before the data were reviewed, the report is not performing its scientific purpose.

Periodic reporting is valuable only when each data lock forces the organisation to reconsider—not merely repeat—its benefit–risk position.
