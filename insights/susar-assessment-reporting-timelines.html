SUSAR Assessment and Reporting: Seriousness, Causality, Expectedness and 7-/15-Day Timelines

Understand SUSAR criteria, seriousness, causality, RSI expectedness, unblinding and 7-/15-day expedited reporting in clinical trials.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

A fatal event is not automatically a SUSAR. An unexpected event is not automatically a SUSAR. Even a serious and unexpected event is not a SUSAR unless a causal relationship with the investigational product is reasonably suspected.

Urgency matters—but speed without correct classification creates regulatory noise and can distort the emerging safety profile.

A SUSAR is not a synonym for an alarming event. It is a conclusion built from seriousness, suspected causality and unexpectedness.

Regulatory foundation: ICH E2A; EMA clinical-trial safety reporting; EU Clinical Trials Regulation; FDA Sponsor Safety Guidance

The three-part SUSAR test

First, determine whether the event meets seriousness criteria. Second, assess whether there is a reasonable possibility of causal relationship. Third, compare the reaction’s nature and severity with the applicable RSI.

All three must be supported before the SUSAR conclusion.

Seriousness

Document the exact criterion and supporting facts. Severe intensity alone is insufficient; medically important events can qualify even without hospitalisation when intervention is needed to prevent a serious outcome.

Fatal and life-threatening cases require particularly rapid triage.

Causality

Consider temporal relationship, mechanism, dose, dechallenge, rechallenge, alternative causes, disease, concomitant therapies and class evidence. Retain investigator and sponsor opinions.

Under-reporting cannot be justified solely by sponsor disagreement where applicable rules require reporting based on either reasonable assessment.

Expectedness and the RSI

Expectedness is a regulatory comparison, not whether a clinician could imagine the event. Use the controlled RSI applicable to the trial, region and assessment date.

Differences in specificity, severity or outcome may render a reaction unexpected even when a related concept is listed.

Seven- and fifteen-day reporting

Fatal or life-threatening SUSARs are generally reported as soon as possible and no later than seven calendar days for the initial report under EU/ICH-aligned frameworks, with required follow-up handled under applicable rules. Other SUSARs generally follow a fifteen-day window.

These are regulatory maximums, not operational targets. Destination, clock start and follow-up differ by jurisdiction.

Blinded trials

The sponsor needs a controlled mechanism to determine treatment assignment where required for reporting while limiting access to preserve trial integrity. Placebo cases and comparator responsibilities require protocol- and region-specific handling.

Every unblinding action should be attributable and justified.

Follow-up, amendment and downgrade

Submit material follow-up without waiting for a perfect final narrative. If later evidence changes diagnosis, seriousness, causality or expectedness, update the report transparently rather than erasing the earlier decision.

A downgrade does not make the original good-faith assessment improper.

Communicating new risk

Expedited submission alone does not protect participants. Investigators and ethics bodies may require prompt information when it materially affects conduct, consent or safety measures.

Avoid indiscriminate distribution of unassessed single cases that creates alert fatigue.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Seriousness criterion evidenced.
  • Causality medically reasoned.
  • Correct RSI version used.
  • Nature and severity compared.
  • Clock start documented.
  • Fatal/life-threatening cases prioritised.
  • Unblinding controlled.
  • Destinations regionally mapped.
  • Follow-up and acknowledgements tracked.
  • Investigator communication is risk-based.

Frequently asked questions

What does SUSAR stand for?

Suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction.

Are all fatal SAEs SUSARs?

No. Causality and unexpectedness must also be assessed.

What is the seven-day rule?

Fatal or life-threatening SUSARs generally require initial expedited reporting as soon as possible and no later than seven calendar days under applicable frameworks.

What is the fifteen-day rule?

Other qualifying SUSARs generally require reporting within fifteen calendar days, subject to regional requirements.

Can expectedness be assessed from general medical knowledge?

No. Regulatory expectedness is assessed against the applicable controlled RSI.

Conclusion

SUSAR reporting is credible when the receiver can see the evidence behind each word: suspected, unexpected, serious and reaction.

The goal is not to maximise report volume. It is to communicate important new risks quickly enough to protect participants without weakening the signal with poorly assessed noise.

Fast reporting protects the clock; disciplined assessment protects the science.
