Development Safety Update Report: A Complete DSUR Preparation Guide

A complete DSUR guide covering DIBD, annual planning, exposure, case tabulations, safety assessment, benefit–risk conclusions and QC.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

A DSUR can contain every required section and still fail its purpose. Tables may reconcile, case listings may be complete and submissions may be on time—yet the report may never answer the only question that matters: has the risk to trial participants changed?

The DSUR is not an annual archive. It is the sponsor’s documented safety judgement across the development programme.

The value of a DSUR is not the volume of data assembled. It is the quality of the decision that emerges from the data.

Regulatory foundation: ICH E2F; EMA clinical-trial safety reporting; EU Clinical Trials Regulation; CTIS Sponsor Handbook

What is a DSUR?

The Development Safety Update Report is the ICH common standard for annual safety reporting for investigational drugs, including marketed products under further study. It summarises interval findings, cumulative experience and the sponsor’s evolving assessment of risk.

Regional submission requirements and recipients must be mapped separately.

Development international birth date and reporting period

The DIBD anchors the annual cycle. Data lock, reporting interval, submission due dates and first/last report logic should be controlled across regions and studies.

Changes or administrative cut-offs must not create gaps or double-counted periods.

Governance and planning

Assign an accountable author, medical lead, regulatory lead, data owners, reviewers and approver. Begin with a content plan, source inventory, prior commitments, signal status and known data limitations.

Late data collection compresses analysis and turns scientific review into document assembly.

Data sources and reconciliation

Integrate clinical-trial exposure, interval and cumulative cases, deaths, withdrawals, study status, nonclinical findings, literature, regulatory actions, manufacturing information where safety-relevant and significant external evidence.

Reconcile safety database, clinical database, study listings, exposure calculations, SUSAR submissions and prior DSUR totals.

Estimated exposure

Present interval and cumulative exposure with methods, assumptions and limitations. Where possible, stratify meaningfully by treatment, dose, population or programme context without compromising the blind.

A precise-looking denominator based on weak assumptions should be described honestly.

Case information and summary tabulations

Serious adverse reactions and relevant cases should be presented consistently, with clear coding versions and case cut-off rules. Explain material differences from previous reports.

Listings are evidence inputs; they are not the analysis.

Significant findings and actions

Discuss completed and ongoing studies, nonclinical information, literature, other DSURs, lack of efficacy where safety-relevant and important manufacturing changes. Describe actions taken for safety reasons and their rationale.

Connect findings across sources rather than reporting them in isolated sections.

Overall safety assessment

Evaluate identified and potential risks, new information on known risks, populations, dose, duration, interactions and uncertainties. Address whether the investigator’s brochure, protocol, consent, monitoring or development plan should change.

State disagreements and evidence limitations openly.

Benefit–risk conclusions

The conclusion should integrate efficacy or anticipated benefit with cumulative risk in the development context. It should identify emerging issues, planned investigations and risk controls with accountable timelines.

‘The benefit–risk remains favourable’ is not sufficient without the reasoning that supports it.

Quality control and inspection readiness

Verify cross-section consistency, table totals, RSI version, DIBD, submission history, actions, references and sign-off. Preserve datasets, queries, decisions and approvals used to create the report.

An inspector should be able to reproduce the important conclusions from governed sources.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • DIBD and reporting period verified.
  • Regional requirements mapped.
  • Prior DSUR commitments reviewed.
  • Study status and exposure reconcile.
  • Case counts match governed sources.
  • SUSAR and submission data reconcile.
  • Signals and actions are current.
  • New nonclinical and literature evidence assessed.
  • Benefit–risk reasoning is explicit.
  • Limitations and missing data disclosed.
  • Review and approval are attributable.
  • Post-submission commitments are tracked.

Frequently asked questions

How often is a DSUR prepared?

Generally annually, anchored to the development international birth date, subject to regional requirements.

What does a DSUR cover?

The worldwide development programme for the investigational drug, integrating interval and cumulative safety information and benefit–risk assessment.

Is a DSUR only a case-line-listing report?

No. Listings and tabulations support the central purpose: cumulative safety and benefit–risk evaluation.

Can one DSUR cover multiple indications?

ICH E2F generally supports one DSUR for one investigational drug across indications where appropriate, with meaningful stratification and explanation.

What is the biggest DSUR quality risk?

A report that compiles complete data but fails to analyse whether the safety profile or participant-protection measures should change.

Conclusion

A strong DSUR lets leadership, investigators and regulators see not only what happened during the year, but what the total evidence now means.

Its credibility comes from reconciled data, transparent uncertainty and actions that follow the conclusion.

The DSUR closes a reporting period only after it opens the next safety decision.
