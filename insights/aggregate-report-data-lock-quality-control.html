Aggregate Safety Reporting

Aggregate Report Data Lock, Reconciliation and Quality Control

Learn data-lock governance, dataset reconciliation, query validation and quality control for PBRER, PSUR and DSUR reports.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

Most aggregate-report failures do not begin in medical writing. They begin earlier—in an undefined query, an unreconciled dataset, a changed MedDRA version or a late source delivered after the data lock.

By the time the inconsistency reaches the conclusion, it looks authoritative.

A report cannot be scientifically stronger than the data package beneath it.

Regulatory foundation: ICH E2C(R2); ICH E2F; GVP VII

Data-lock governance

Define the DLP, timezone, late-arriving data policy, version freeze, refresh rules and exception approval. Every contributor must use the same reporting interval.

Source reconciliation

Reconcile safety database cases with submissions, partners, clinical systems, literature, signal trackers, regulatory actions and exposure sources.

Query specifications

Record inclusion criteria, product scope, case versions, seriousness, event groupings, MedDRA version, nullifications, duplicates and output validation.

Table-to-text consistency

Numbers repeated across sections should derive from controlled outputs. Manual transcription creates preventable error. Narrative claims should cite the exact table, listing or assessment.

Medical and regulatory QC

Technical QC confirms counts and formatting. Medical QC challenges interpretation, emerging patterns, missing context and conclusion strength. Regulatory QC confirms format, regional content and submission requirements.

Managing late changes

Material post-lock changes require impact assessment, controlled refresh and reapproval. Silent replacement of tables after review destroys traceability.

The scientific questions every aggregate team must answer

The reporting period should be interrogated, not merely described. What changed in exposure? Which events increased or changed clinically? Did new studies alter understanding of a risk or benefit? Did a signal assessment change the safety specification? Did regulatory action in one country create implications elsewhere? Did risk-minimisation data show that an intervention worked—or failed?

A useful report distinguishes new information from new interpretation. Sometimes no new event is identified, yet cumulative evidence changes severity, frequency, susceptible population, preventability or clinical management. That change can be as important as discovery of a new reaction.

  • Explain important interval changes against cumulative context.
  • State limitations of exposure and case ascertainment.
  • Reconcile signal conclusions with RMP and product information.
  • Identify concrete actions arising from the evaluation.

Authoring governance and cross-functional accountability

Aggregate reports are assembled across safety operations, medical review, biostatistics, epidemiology, clinical development, regulatory affairs, labelling and risk management. The author should not be forced to discover at final review that these functions used different data cuts or product scopes.

A controlled source matrix should define each input, system of record, owner, cut-off, format, reconciliation requirement and approval. Review comments should be resolved with documented rationale, especially when they change risk classification or benefit–risk conclusions.

  • Freeze governed datasets before final medical interpretation.
  • Use one controlled source for repeated numbers.
  • Record material judgement changes between drafts.
  • Link conclusions to follow-up actions and accountable owners.

A practical decision scenario

Consider a reporting period in which spontaneous case counts for a known risk rise by 60%. The increase appears alarming until exposure data show a major market expansion and a communication campaign that stimulated reporting. At the same time, the proportion of cases involving hospitalisation has increased in one vulnerable population. A strong aggregate report does not choose between 'more reports' and 'no change.' It separates reporting volume from clinical pattern, evaluates population-specific severity and determines whether targeted risk action is required.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • One controlled DLP is used.
  • Queries are versioned.
  • Sources reconcile.
  • MedDRA and product dictionaries are fixed.
  • Tables trace to outputs.
  • Medical conclusions trace to evidence.
  • Late changes are assessed and approved.

Frequently asked questions

What is a data lock point?

The defined cut-off date for data included in a periodic report.

Why reconcile datasets?

To prevent missing, duplicated or inconsistent evidence across sections and submissions.

What is aggregate-report QC?

Technical, medical and regulatory verification of data, interpretation, format and compliance.

Can tables be refreshed after DLP?

Only through a governed exception and impact-assessment process.

Conclusion

Data governance is not separate from medical quality. Every untraceable number weakens the conclusion built on it.

When two sections disagree, the problem is not formatting. It is that the organisation no longer has one governed version of the safety truth.
