Pharmacovigilance Reconciliation: Cases, Submissions, Literature and Partner Records

Learn PV reconciliation for cases, follow-ups, submissions, literature, partners, discrepancies and automated matching.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

Two partners can each confirm that their database is complete and still hold different cases. The sender may count transmissions; the receiver may count accepted imports. Follow-ups may be separate rows in one system and versions of one case in another.

Without controlled reconciliation, both parties can be internally correct and collectively non-compliant.

Reconciliation is not comparing totals. It is proving that every expected safety record reached the correct destination and was resolved.

Regulatory foundation: GVP Module I; GVP Module II; GVP Module VI; EMA electronic reporting

What reconciliation controls

Reconciliation tests completeness and consistency across organisational or system boundaries. It can cover cases, follow-ups, literature outputs, submissions, acknowledgements, product complaints, medical-information records and other defined safety deliverables.

Define the reconciliation population

Specify period, products, countries, sources, initial and follow-up versions, nullifications, duplicates, test cases, transferred dates and unique identifiers. Total counts without a shared population are not comparable.

Case-level matching

Use stable identifiers plus patient, reporter, product, event, country and receipt information where permitted. Match initial and follow-up chronology, not merely case numbers.

Potential duplicates require investigation rather than forced closure to make totals agree.

Submission reconciliation

Compare safety-database obligations with generated messages, gateway transmissions, receiver acknowledgements, acceptance, rejection and retransmission. A sent message is not necessarily an accepted submission.

Literature reconciliation

Reconcile search runs, screened records, included articles, valid cases, duplicates and transfer to the safety database. Local and global processes should not silently screen the same article differently.

Frequency and risk

Frequency should reflect exchange volume, seriousness, reporting timelines, partner performance and system risk. Waiting quarterly to reconcile high-volume expedited cases may detect failure too late.

Discrepancy classification

Classify missing record, late transfer, mismatched data, duplicate, version error, scope error, submission error and documentation gap. Assess patient-safety and regulatory impact, not only administrative cause.

Closure and evidence

Each discrepancy needs owner, investigation, correction, impact assessment, due date and closure evidence. Unresolved discrepancies should remain visible to governance and metrics.

Automation and limitations

Automated matching improves speed but requires validated rules, exception queues and human review. Exact matching may miss duplicates; fuzzy matching may create false pairs. Both require controlled thresholds.

A practical scenario

The sender lists 100 records and the receiver lists 100, so the reconciliation is marked complete. Later, review finds that two sender cases never arrived and two receiver cases were unrelated local reports. Count equality concealed four discrepancies.

This is why record-level matching and exception investigation are essential.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Population and period are shared.
  • Initial and follow-up versions are defined.
  • Record-level matching is performed.
  • Submissions include acknowledgement status.
  • Literature outputs reconcile with case creation.
  • Discrepancies have impact assessments.
  • Late exchange is measured from correct awareness.
  • Exceptions remain open until evidenced closure.
  • Automated rules are validated.

Frequently asked questions

What is PV reconciliation?

A controlled comparison proving completeness and consistency of safety information across parties, systems or processes.

Is comparing case totals sufficient?

No. Equal totals can contain different records; case-level matching is needed.

How often should reconciliation occur?

According to risk, volume and applicable timelines, with sufficient frequency to detect failure before harm or non-compliance becomes irreversible.

Should rejected submissions be reconciled?

Yes. Transmission, acknowledgement, acceptance, rejection and retransmission should be visible.

Can reconciliation be automated?

Yes, with validated matching logic, exception management and human review.

Conclusion

Reconciliation is the evidence that the safety-information chain remained intact. It does not assume that transfer worked because no one reported a problem. It tests the interface and documents the result.

If two systems agree only at the total-count level, they have not reconciled the safety data—they have compared arithmetic.
