Pharmacovigilance Reconciliation: Cases, Submissions, Literature and Partner Records

Learn PV reconciliation for cases, follow-ups, submissions, literature, partners, discrepancies and automated matching.

By Dr. Surya, Doctorate in Pharmacy 2026-07-234 min read

I've seen two partners each pull up their own database, confirm it's complete, and still be holding different sets of cases. The sender is counting transmissions sent. The receiver is counting imports accepted. A follow-up that's one row in one system might be logged as a separate version of the case in the other.

Without controlled reconciliation, both sides can be entirely correct by their own records and still be collectively non-compliant. That gap is invisible until someone actually goes looking for it.

Reconciliation isn't comparing totals. It's proving that every safety record that was supposed to arrive actually reached the right destination and got resolved.

Regulatory foundation: GVP Module I; GVP Module II; GVP Module VI; EMA electronic reporting

What reconciliation controls

Reconciliation is a completeness and consistency test across whatever boundary you're checking — organisational, systemic, or both. It can span cases, follow-ups, literature outputs, submissions, acknowledgements, product complaints, medical-information records, and any other defined safety deliverable.

Define the reconciliation population

Pin down the period, the products, the countries, the sources, initial versus follow-up versions, nullifications, duplicates, test cases, transfer dates, and unique identifiers before you compare anything. Two totals that weren't drawn from the same defined population aren't actually comparable, no matter how close the numbers look.

Case-level matching

Match on stable identifiers, plus patient, reporter, product, event, country and receipt information wherever that's permitted. Match the chronology of initial and follow-up reports — not just the case numbers attached to them.

When you spot a potential duplicate, investigate it properly. Don't just force a closure so the totals line up.

Submission reconciliation

Check safety-database obligations against generated messages, gateway transmissions, receiver acknowledgements, acceptance, rejection, and any retransmission. A message that was sent is not automatically a submission that was accepted — that distinction matters more than it gets credit for.

Literature reconciliation

Reconcile search runs, screened records, included articles, valid cases, duplicates, and the actual transfer into the safety database. Local and global teams shouldn't be silently screening the same article two different ways without anyone noticing.

Frequency and risk

How often you reconcile should track exchange volume, seriousness, reporting timelines, partner performance, and overall system risk. If you're only reconciling quarterly on a high-volume expedited-case relationship, you may well find out about a failure long after it mattered.

Discrepancy classification

Classify what you find: missing record, late transfer, mismatched data, duplicate, version error, scope error, submission error, documentation gap. Weigh the patient-safety and regulatory impact of each one — not just the administrative cause behind it.

Closure and evidence

Every discrepancy needs an owner, an investigation, a correction, an impact assessment, a due date, and evidence of closure. Anything still unresolved should stay visible to governance and in your metrics — not quietly drop off the list.

Automation and limitations

Automated matching speeds things up, but it needs validated rules, an exception queue, and someone actually reviewing the exceptions. Exact matching can miss real duplicates; fuzzy matching can invent false pairs that aren't actually the same case. Both need controlled thresholds, not a default setting left untouched.

A practical scenario

The sender lists 100 records. The receiver lists 100. Reconciliation gets marked complete on the spot. A later review finds two sender cases that never actually arrived, and two receiver cases that turned out to be unrelated local reports entirely. Matching totals had quietly concealed four real discrepancies.

That's exactly why record-level matching and genuine exception investigation aren't optional steps — they're the whole point.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Population and period are shared.
  • Initial and follow-up versions are defined.
  • Record-level matching is performed.
  • Submissions include acknowledgement status.
  • Literature outputs reconcile with case creation.
  • Discrepancies have impact assessments.
  • Late exchange is measured from correct awareness.
  • Exceptions remain open until evidenced closure.
  • Automated rules are validated.

Frequently asked questions

What is PV reconciliation?

A controlled comparison proving completeness and consistency of safety information across parties, systems or processes.

Is comparing case totals sufficient?

No. Equal totals can contain different records; case-level matching is needed.

How often should reconciliation occur?

According to risk, volume and applicable timelines, with sufficient frequency to detect failure before harm or non-compliance becomes irreversible.

Should rejected submissions be reconciled?

Yes. Transmission, acknowledgement, acceptance, rejection and retransmission should be visible.

Can reconciliation be automated?

Yes, with validated matching logic, exception management and human review.

Conclusion

Reconciliation is the evidence that the safety-information chain actually held together end to end. It never assumes a transfer worked just because nobody flagged a problem. It tests the interface directly and documents what it finds.

If two systems only agree at the level of a total count, they haven't reconciled the safety data. They've just compared arithmetic.
