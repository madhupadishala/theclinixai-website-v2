Pharmacoepidemiology Study Designs: Cohort, Case-Control and Self-Controlled Methods

Compare cohort, case-control, self-controlled, cross-sectional, registry and drug-utilisation designs for medicine-safety research.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

The same database can produce three different risk estimates—not because one analysis is dishonest, but because each design asks a different question, selects different patients and handles time differently.

Study design is not a formatting choice. It defines the comparison that makes inference possible.

Choose the design after defining the causal question—not after opening the database.

Regulatory foundation: ICH M14; ENCePP Methods Guide; GVP Module VIII; ENCePP Code of Conduct

The design follows the estimand

Define the population, treatment strategies, outcome, time horizon and effect measure. Decide whether the objective is incidence description, comparative association, acute risk-window evaluation or utilisation.

No method compensates for an undefined question.

Cohort studies

Cohorts classify participants by exposure and follow them for outcomes. They estimate incidence and support several outcomes from one exposure. New-user active-comparator designs can improve alignment and reduce confounding.

Key risks include confounding by indication, immortal time, informative censoring and exposure misclassification.

Case-control studies

Case-control studies select people with an outcome and controls from the source population, then compare prior exposure. They are efficient for rare outcomes and can examine multiple exposures.

Control sampling must represent the exposure distribution of the population that produced the cases. Odds ratios approximate rate ratios under appropriate sampling.

Nested case-control designs

Sampling cases and controls within a defined cohort preserves source-population clarity and can reduce measurement burden for expensive covariates or validation.

Matching factors and analysis must remain aligned.

Self-controlled case series

Self-controlled case series compare predefined risk and control periods within people who experienced the event, controlling for time-invariant confounders. They suit transient exposures and acute outcomes.

Assumptions include event-independent exposure and observation; event-dependent death or exposure can seriously bias results.

Case-crossover design

Case-crossover studies compare exposure during a hazard window with the same person’s control windows. They are useful for transient exposures with abrupt outcomes.

Time trends, carry-over and exposure persistence require careful control.

Cross-sectional and ecological studies

Cross-sectional studies describe exposure and outcome at a point or period but have limited temporality. Ecological studies compare groups and risk ecological fallacy.

These designs can generate hypotheses or characterise use but rarely resolve individual causal safety questions.

Registries and drug-utilisation studies

Registries organise longitudinal information around disease, product or exposure. Drug-utilisation studies describe prescribing, adherence, switching, off-label use or risk-minimisation uptake.

A data platform is not itself a study design; analytic questions still require a defined comparison.

Selecting the comparator

Comparators should reflect the clinical decision and reduce differences in indication, disease severity and healthcare contact. Non-users may be inappropriate when treatment indication itself predicts outcome.

Active comparators and new-user designs are often useful but not universally feasible.

Triangulation

Confidence increases when designs with different bias structures reach compatible conclusions. Discordance should trigger investigation rather than selective reporting.

Triangulation combines evidence; it does not average away important methodological differences.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Estimand is defined.
  • Design matches exposure and outcome timing.
  • Source population is identifiable.
  • Comparator reflects the clinical question.
  • New-user design considered.
  • Time zero aligned across groups.
  • Major bias pathways mapped.
  • Assumptions are testable where possible.
  • Sensitivity analyses target uncertainty.
  • Interpretation remains design-specific.

Frequently asked questions

Which design is best for medicine safety?

There is no universally best design; selection depends on the question, outcome frequency, exposure pattern, confounding and data.

When is a cohort study useful?

When incidence, temporal follow-up and multiple outcomes after exposure are important.

Why use a case-control study?

It is efficient for rare outcomes and allows detailed assessment of prior exposures and covariates.

What do self-controlled designs control?

They inherently control fixed individual characteristics but remain vulnerable to time-varying confounding and violated timing assumptions.

Can several designs be used for one signal?

Yes. Triangulation across methods and sources can strengthen interpretation when biases differ.

Conclusion

The credibility of a pharmacoepidemiology result begins before the first model runs. It begins when the investigator chooses who is compared, when follow-up starts and which assumptions make that comparison meaningful.

Methods do not create certainty. They structure uncertainty so that the conclusion can be tested.

The right design does not guarantee the right answer—but the wrong design can make the answer irrelevant before analysis begins.
