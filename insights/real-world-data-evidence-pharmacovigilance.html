Pharmacoepidemiology & Real-World Evidence

Real-World Data and Real-World Evidence in Pharmacovigilance

Learn RWD and RWE fundamentals, fit-for-use assessment, exposure and outcome measurement, confounding, linkage and reproducibility in PV.

By Wany Williams, BDS 2026-07-234 min read

I've pulled up databases boasting forty million patients that sounded impressive right up until I checked the details — the safety outcome only existed in free text, exposure end dates couldn't actually be determined, and patients kept vanishing every time their insurance changed.

Scale is the thing you can see at a glance. Fitness for purpose is the thing you actually have to check.

Real-world data become evidence only after the data, design and analysis are shown to be fit for the question.

Regulatory foundation: ICH M14; ENCePP Methods Guide; GVP Module VIII; ENCePP Code of Conduct

RWD is not automatically RWE

RWD may come from electronic health records, claims, registries, dispensing systems, devices or patient sources. RWE is the inference generated from fit-for-use RWD through a transparent study.

The same source can be suitable for one question and unsuitable for another.

Data relevance

Assess whether the source contains the population, exposure, comparator, outcome, covariates, follow-up and sample size required. Consider healthcare setting and whether results can generalise to the target population.

A missing key confounder cannot always be replaced by a proxy.

Data reliability

Understand collection purpose, provenance, transformation, coding, quality checks, completeness, consistency, timeliness and linkage. Review changes in software, reimbursement, clinical practice and coding over time.

Reliability is an evidence package, not a vendor statement.

Exposure measurement

Prescription, dispensing and administration capture different facts. Define initiation, continuation, switching, dose, adherence and grace periods. Misclassified time can create immortal-time and time-window bias.

Sensitivity analyses should test reasonable alternative exposure definitions.

Outcome phenotyping

Use validated algorithms where available or justify validation in the intended source. Combine codes, laboratory values, procedures, notes and adjudication when needed.

Positive predictive value alone does not describe missed outcomes; sensitivity also affects interpretation.

Confounders and missing data

Identify causal variables before model selection. Distinguish true absence from non-capture. Describe imputation, missingness assumptions, proxies and residual confounding.

More covariates do not guarantee better control when measurements are poor.

Data linkage and privacy

Linkage may improve clinical completeness but can introduce selection and matching error. Define deterministic or probabilistic methods, linkage quality and unmatched populations.

Govern consent, privacy, access, retention and permitted secondary use.

Reproducibility and transparency

Preserve data specifications, code lists, analytic code, software versions, transformations and protocol history. Where data cannot be shared, provide enough metadata and methods for independent evaluation.

Undocumented cleaning choices can change results as materially as the statistical model.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Source matches the target population.
  • Exposure can be measured over time.
  • Outcome phenotype is validated or justified.
  • Key confounders are captured.
  • Follow-up and censoring are interpretable.
  • Coding changes are assessed.
  • Linkage quality is quantified.
  • Missing-data assumptions are explicit.
  • Transformations and code lists are retained.
  • Limitations are reflected in conclusions.

Frequently asked questions

What is real-world data?

Data relating to health status or healthcare delivery routinely collected from sources outside traditional randomised trials.

What is real-world evidence?

Clinical evidence generated by analysing fit-for-use RWD using an appropriate, transparent design.

Is a larger database always better?

No. Relevance, reliability and measurement quality are more important than size alone.

What makes RWD fit for use?

Sufficient relevance and reliability for the specific regulatory or scientific question.

Can EHR data prove causality?

Not automatically. Causal inference depends on design, measurement, bias control and assumptions—not the source label.

Conclusion

Real-world evidence earns credibility when you can trace and challenge every step, from how the source data was captured to the conclusion at the end.

The database just supplies observations. Whether those observations can actually support a safety decision comes down to the scientific governance behind them.

Do not ask how much data you have until you know how much of the required truth the data can actually capture.
