PV Quality Management System

Pharmacovigilance KPIs and Compliance Metrics: What Safety Leaders Should Measure

Learn how to design PV KPIs for cases, submissions, signals, aggregate reports, CAPA, vendors and inspection-ready oversight.

By Wany Williams, BDS 2026-07-234 min read

A green dashboard can hide a failing pharmacovigilance system. Ninety-nine per cent compliance sounds strong until the missed one per cent includes fatal cases, overdue signals or rejected regulatory submissions.

Metrics become dangerous when they reassure leadership without representing patient-safety risk.

A KPI is valuable only when it changes a decision before the compliance failure becomes irreversible.

Regulatory foundation: GVP Module I; GVP Module II; GVP Module III; GVP Module IV

What PV metrics should accomplish

Metrics should monitor compliance, quality, workload, timeliness, control effectiveness and emerging risk. They should support QPPV and management oversight, resource decisions, escalation and continuous improvement.

A metric that nobody acts on is reporting—not control.

Leading and lagging indicators

Lagging indicators describe failures that already occurred: late submissions, critical errors or inspection findings. Leading indicators expose growing risk: backlog, ageing actions, rising rework, delayed follow-up or increasing gateway rejection.

A balanced framework needs both.

ICSR and submission metrics

Measure receipt-to-entry, processing, QC, medical review, due-date performance, gateway acceptance, rejection, retransmission, backlog and follow-up. Denominators must include the full obligation.

Segment by seriousness, source, country, product, vendor and root cause.

Literature, signals and aggregate reports

Track search execution, missed searches, review ageing, valid cases, signal validation and assessment timelines, overdue actions, aggregate milestones, data reconciliation, review cycles and submission acceptance.

Cycle time alone should not reward premature closure.

Quality and CAPA metrics

Monitor critical and major error rates, repeat findings, deviation ageing, root-cause quality, CAPA implementation, effectiveness failure and recurrence. Closure count is not proof of improvement.

Vendor and affiliate metrics

Partner measures should cover exchange timeliness, reconciliation discrepancies, case quality, submission responsibility, deviations, corrective actions and governance attendance. Measures must align with agreements and regulatory risk.

Metric definitions and data integrity

Every KPI requires purpose, numerator, denominator, inclusion/exclusion rules, source system, owner, frequency, threshold, escalation and limitations. Changing a definition requires control and impact assessment.

Manual spreadsheets should not silently become the system of record.

Thresholds and escalation

Thresholds should reflect risk tolerance and regulatory obligation. A threshold is not a target to negotiate after failure. Repeated amber status can be more dangerous than one red event when no action follows.

Escalation should identify who decides, by when and what evidence is required for closure.

Avoiding metric manipulation

Common manipulation includes excluding rejected transmissions, measuring from database entry rather than awareness, resetting ageing after reassignment, averaging away outliers and counting repeat training as CAPA success.

Independent quality review should challenge whether the metric reflects the real obligation.

A practical dashboard architecture

Executive dashboards should show critical risk and trends. Operational dashboards should support daily control. QPPV views should connect compliance with safety-profile and system-risk information.

Drill-down from a green tile to the underlying cases, products and causes should be possible.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Every KPI has a governed definition.
  • Denominators reflect the complete obligation.
  • Leading and lagging indicators are balanced.
  • High-risk outliers are visible.
  • Gateway acceptance—not only transmission—is measured.
  • Quality and CAPA effectiveness are included.
  • Vendors and affiliates are stratified.
  • Thresholds trigger accountable action.
  • Definition changes are controlled.

Frequently asked questions

What are pharmacovigilance KPIs?

Governed measures used to monitor the timeliness, quality, compliance, workload and effectiveness of PV processes.

What is the difference between KPI and KRI?

A KPI measures performance against objectives; a key risk indicator focuses on conditions suggesting increasing risk. In practice, both should support action.

Is on-time submission rate enough?

No. It should include correct destinations, receiver acceptance, rejected messages, case quality and denominator integrity.

What is a leading PV indicator?

A measure such as backlog growth or ageing actions that can signal future failure before a deadline is missed.

How should thresholds be set?

According to regulatory obligation, patient-safety impact, process capability and governance-approved risk tolerance.

Conclusion

The best PV dashboard does not make leaders comfortable. It makes risk visible early enough to act.

When a KPI turns red, the question is not how to recolour the tile. It is what failed, who is accountable and how patient and compliance impact will be controlled.

Measure what can harm the patient or break the obligation—not merely what is easy to count.
