Safety Data Exchange Agreement: Essential Clauses, Timelines and Responsibilities

Learn essential SDEA clauses for scope, day zero, case transfer, submissions, literature, reconciliation, audit and termination.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

An SDEA can be signed by both parties and still fail on day one. It may define a two-day transfer timeline without defining day zero. It may assign submission responsibility without naming countries. It may require reconciliation without specifying what evidence proves completeness.

The signature creates the agreement. Operational precision creates the control.

A safety agreement should answer the question before the case arrives—not while the reporting clock is already running.

Regulatory foundation: GVP Module I; GVP Module II; GVP Module VI; EMA PV system Q&A

Purpose and scope

Define parties, legal entities, products, formulations, territories, lifecycle status, effective date and relationship. Specify whether the agreement covers marketed products, clinical trials, named-patient use or transitional activities.

Definitions that prevent disputes

Define adverse event, adverse reaction, special situation, valid case, awareness, initial information, follow-up, serious, day zero, business day and calendar day. Use applicable regulatory meanings and avoid commercial shorthand.

Case collection and transfer

Specify intake routes, minimum transfer dataset, source-document handling, language, privacy, format, sender, receiver, acknowledgement and escalation for failed transfer.

Transfer rules should include incomplete reports and special situations rather than waiting for full validity.

Follow-up, duplicates and medical information

Define who contacts reporters, who controls follow-up questions, how duplicate suspicion is communicated and how additional information is versioned. Competing follow-up by both parties can confuse reporters and fragment evidence.

Regulatory submission

Assign submission by product, country and case category. Define reportability assessment, message generation, gateway monitoring, acknowledgement, rejection, correction and proof of acceptance.

One party transmitting globally does not automatically cover every local obligation.

Literature and other sources

Assign global and local literature monitoring, search frequencies, databases, local journals, translations, duplicate management and case creation. Also address medical information, product complaints, digital media, market research and patient-support programmes where applicable.

Signals, aggregate reports and risk management

Define access to case data, signal notification, validation, assessment, health-authority communication, PBRER/PSUR or DSUR contributions, data lock points, RMP changes and urgent safety issues.

Reconciliation and compliance

State frequency, dataset, evidence, discrepancy classification, late-case impact, closure timelines and escalation. Metrics should include quality and acceptance—not only number exchanged.

Audit, inspection and CAPA

Define audit rights, inspection support, response timelines, access to records, finding notification, CAPA ownership and effectiveness evidence. Confidentiality cannot prevent a regulated party from meeting inspection obligations.

Change, continuity and termination

Cover contact updates, regulatory change, system changes, business continuity, disaster recovery, data migration, divestment, termination, record retention and open obligations.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Scope names products, territories and entities.
  • Day zero and transfer timelines are defined.
  • Initial and follow-up formats are controlled.
  • Submission destinations and acknowledgements are assigned.
  • Literature and special situations are covered.
  • Signals and aggregate responsibilities are included.
  • Reconciliation method and evidence are defined.
  • Audit, CAPA and inspection support are enforceable.
  • Termination preserves records and open work.

Frequently asked questions

What is an SDEA?

A safety data exchange agreement defines how two or more parties share and govern pharmacovigilance information and responsibilities.

What is the most important SDEA clause?

No single clause is sufficient; scope, awareness, timelines, submission ownership, reconciliation and oversight must work together.

Should incomplete cases be exchanged?

The agreement should reflect applicable obligations and generally avoid delaying potentially relevant safety information while waiting for completeness.

Who owns follow-up?

The agreement should assign ownership and coordination to prevent duplicate contact and fragmented evidence.

What happens after termination?

Open cases, submissions, signals, records, retention and inspection support must transition through a controlled plan.

Conclusion

An SDEA is successful when operations do not need to interpret it under pressure. The roles, clocks, formats and escalation paths are already understood, tested and measurable.

Ambiguity written into an SDEA becomes delay written into the safety process.
