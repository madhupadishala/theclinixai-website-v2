Post-Authorisation Safety Studies: A Complete PASS Design and Compliance Guide

Learn PASS objectives, classification, protocol design, GVP Module VIII requirements, registration, safety reporting and regulatory use.

TheClinixAI PV Science & Operations 2026-07-234 min read

A PASS can be delivered on time, include every required section and still fail its purpose. The protocol may use the available database rather than the necessary data. The outcome may be easy to code but clinically imprecise. The comparator may be convenient but systematically healthier.

Regulatory completion is not scientific success.

A PASS should not begin with ‘Which database do we have?’ It should begin with ‘Which decision must this study inform?’

Regulatory foundation: GVP Module VIII; GVP Module VIII Addendum I; EMA PASS overview; ICH M14

What is a PASS?

A PASS is a post-authorisation study relating to an authorised medicinal product, conducted to identify, characterise or quantify a safety hazard, confirm the safety profile or measure risk-management effectiveness. It may be interventional or non-interventional.

EU procedures differ for imposed and voluntary studies and according to legal basis.

Start with the regulatory and scientific objective

Connect the study to the safety specification, RMP, authority request or emerging evidence. Define whether the objective is descriptive, hypothesis-testing, utilisation-focused or effectiveness-focused.

One protocol should not accumulate unrelated objectives that require incompatible designs.

Classify the study correctly

Determine whether treatment assignment follows normal practice and remains separate from study inclusion. Interventional status changes the governing clinical-trial framework. Document the rationale and obtain regulatory clarification where needed.

Do not label a study non-interventional merely because it uses routine-care sites.

Protocol essentials

Define milestones, background, objectives, design, setting, population, variables, data sources, sample size, analysis, data quality, limitations, protection of participants, management of adverse events and dissemination.

Amendments require rationale, version control and assessment of their impact on interpretation.

Data and design fitness

Evaluate whether exposure, outcome, covariates, follow-up and population are sufficiently captured. Pilot feasibility can expose missingness, coding drift and inadequate sample size before final commitment.

If the data cannot answer the question, a larger sample will produce a more precise wrong answer.

Governance and independence

Define sponsor, study lead, scientific committee, data holder, analysts, medical review, quality oversight and publication rights. Contracts should protect scientific independence, access to data and timely reporting.

Conflicts of interest and sponsor influence should be transparent.

Registration and regulatory submissions

Map protocol, substantial amendment, progress and final-report requirements by legal category and jurisdiction. GVP Module VIII Addendum I provides EU submission recommendations and requirements for non-interventional PASS.

Registration should occur according to applicable rules and before results are known.

Safety information during the study

The protocol should distinguish study endpoints, aggregate findings and reportable individual safety information. Collection and reporting pathways depend on study design, data source and regional requirements.

Avoid duplicate reporting across study and spontaneous systems while preserving due diligence.

Results and action

Report methods, deviations, limitations, numerical results and interpretation without suppressing unfavourable findings. Feed conclusions into RMP, signal, product-information and risk-minimisation governance.

Track commitments arising after final submission.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • Regulatory basis identified.
  • Decision question is explicit.
  • Interventional status justified.
  • Protocol is complete before data analysis.
  • Database feasibility demonstrated.
  • Bias and confounding plans are pre-specified.
  • Governance protects scientific integrity.
  • Submission milestones are controlled.
  • Safety-reporting pathway is defined.
  • Results feed accountable PV action.

Frequently asked questions

What is the purpose of a PASS?

To generate post-authorisation evidence that identifies, characterises or quantifies safety concerns, confirms safety profiles or evaluates risk-management measures.

Are all PASS non-interventional?

No. PASS can be interventional or non-interventional; the classification determines the governing framework.

Who can impose a PASS?

Competent authorities may impose studies under applicable legal procedures; MAHs may also conduct voluntary PASS.

Does every PASS require regulatory protocol review?

Requirements depend on legal category and jurisdiction.

Can a PASS use secondary healthcare data?

Yes, when the data are fit for the question and the design and analysis address important biases.

Conclusion

A PASS is successful when it reduces uncertainty enough to support a real regulatory or clinical decision.

The report is the deliverable. Credible evidence—and the safety action it enables—is the outcome.

Do not design the study around available data until you have proved that those data can answer the safety question.
