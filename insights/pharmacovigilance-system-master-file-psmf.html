PV Quality Management System

Pharmacovigilance System Master File: PSMF Structure, Maintenance and Inspection Readiness

Learn PSMF structure, QPPV oversight, annexes, vendor mapping, system descriptions, maintenance and inspection readiness.

By Wany Williams, BDS 2026-07-234 min read

I've reviewed PSMFs that were formatted perfectly and still weren't reliable underneath. The org chart was six months out of date. A vendor was doing case processing without ever showing up in the subcontract list. An unresolved critical audit finding was just... missing from the notes. Product responsibility didn't match what the annex said.

At that point you're not looking at a master description of the pharmacovigilance system anymore. You're looking at a well-formatted document describing a different system than the one actually running.

The PSMF is not a regulatory brochure. It is the inspection map of the pharmacovigilance system.

Regulatory foundation: GVP Module II; GVP Module I; EMA PV system Q&A; GVP Module III

What the PSMF is designed to do

The PSMF provides a detailed description of the PV system used for one or more authorised medicinal products. It supports oversight, audit planning and inspection by showing how responsibilities, processes, systems and records connect.

The MAH retains responsibility for completeness and accuracy even when maintenance activities are delegated.

Core body and annex architecture

The PSMF typically describes the QPPV, organisational structure, safety-data sources, computerised systems and databases, PV processes, system performance, quality system and document or record management. Annexes hold dynamic detail that must reconcile with the body.

  • QPPV information and back-up arrangements.
  • Organisational structure and interfaces.
  • Safety-data sources and collection routes.
  • Computerised systems and validated status.
  • Process descriptions and compliance information.
  • Quality system, audits and CAPA notes.
  • Products, contracts, procedures and performance annexes.

QPPV oversight and authority

The description should show how the QPPV obtains information, accesses systems, influences the quality system and remains aware of safety profiles and compliance. Naming the QPPV without demonstrating operational access is not adequate.

Back-up arrangements must work in practice and cover absence, emergencies and cross-time-zone communication.

Products, territories and delegated activities

Product and territory coverage must remain accurate. Subcontracts should identify the delegated activity, products and territories. Safety agreements and vendor arrangements must align with the PSMF.

I see the same failure mode over and over: fragmented ownership. Procurement holds the contract, legal holds the agreement, operations owns the workflow — and the PSMF owner finds out about the change months after the fact, if at all.

Computerised systems and data flows

The PSMF should identify key safety systems, their purpose, location, ownership, validation status and interfaces. The description should include outsourced platforms and important spreadsheets or tools where they influence regulated activity.

Data-flow descriptions should show how information moves from source to database, review, submission, signal and aggregate use.

Maintaining the PSMF as a living file

Change control should be triggered by QPPV changes, organisational changes, acquisitions, new products, vendors, systems, processes, locations, audit findings and significant compliance issues.

Annex updates may be frequent, but frequency is not control. Changes require ownership, effective dates, history and periodic reconciliation.

Audit findings and CAPA notes

Significant audit findings and associated corrective and preventive action status must be represented according to applicable requirements. Removal requires objective evidence of completion—not administrative closure.

The PSMF should not hide uncomfortable evidence. Its purpose is to make the system transparent enough to govern.

Inspection provision and retrieval

The organisation should be able to provide the PSMF within applicable requirements and in a usable form. Links, annexes and referenced documents should work. Inspectors should not have to navigate broken references or conflicting versions.

Routine retrieval tests are more valuable than assuming the document can be assembled when requested.

Practical reconciliation controls

Quarterly or risk-based reconciliation should compare PSMF content with regulatory product data, contracts, vendor inventories, validated-system inventories, organisational records, SOP lists, audit trackers and KPI definitions.

Every discrepancy should have an owner, impact assessment and due date.

Inspection-ready checklist

  • PSMF scope matches authorised products.
  • QPPV and back-up details are current.
  • Organisation and data flows reflect practice.
  • Vendors, contracts and territories reconcile.
  • System inventory and validation status are current.
  • Procedures and KPI annexes are controlled.
  • Audit/CAPA notes are accurate.
  • Change history is attributable.
  • Retrieval is periodically tested.

Frequently asked questions

What is a PSMF?

A detailed, maintained description of the pharmacovigilance system used by an MAH for one or more authorised medicinal products.

Who is responsible for PSMF accuracy?

The MAH retains responsibility, even if maintenance is delegated.

Is the PSMF submitted routinely?

Regional requirements vary; in the EU it is maintained and made available to competent authorities upon request, with required summary/location information managed through applicable processes.

How often should it be updated?

Whenever relevant system information changes, supported by ongoing control and periodic reconciliation.

Can unresolved audit findings be omitted?

No. Applicable significant findings and CAPA status must be represented transparently under governing requirements.

Conclusion

The strongest PSMF isn't the longest one. It's the one that lets an independent reviewer actually understand the real system, find the evidence, and see where risk is genuinely being controlled.

When the PSMF and actual practice disagree, inspectors believe the practice every time.

The PSMF should never be prepared for inspection. It should already be accurate because the system is continuously governed.
